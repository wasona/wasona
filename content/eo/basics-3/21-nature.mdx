## Ingredients

Bread products are often used to hold together several different ingredients:

<List>
  <Sentence sp="󱥋󱤧󱤓󱤉󱥢" sl="pan li jo <u>e soweli</u>." m="La pico enhavas viandon." />
  <Sentence sp="󱥋󱤧󱤓󱤉󱤚" sl="pan li jo <u>e kili</u>." m="La pico enhavas legomojn." />
  <Sentence sp="󱥋󱤧󱤓󱤉󱥸" sl="pan li jo <u>e namako</u>." m="La pico enhavas spicaĵojn." />
</List>

{/* A note for translators */}
{/*
  Your culture may have a different popular food that consists of meat, vegetables, and condiments, put into or onto bread. You are free to replace pizza with that food.
*/}

But how do we put them together into a single sentence? Well, each of them is the *object* of a sentence. A sentence can have *several objects*: several **󱤉 e**-phrases, which simply go one after the other:

<List>
  <Sentence
    sl="pan li jo e soweli e kili e namako."
    m="The pizza contains meat, vegetables, (and) condiments."
  />
</List>

This is not special to just **󱤉 e**! You can combine several related ideas into one sentence with **󱤧 li**, **󱤡 la**, and prepositions too. For now let's just look at **󱤧 li**:

<List>
  <Sentence sl="jan li alasa e kala." m="La homo ĉasas fiŝojn. (= la homo fiŝkaptas)." />
  <Sentence sl="jan li seli e ona." m="La homo kuiras ilin." />
  <Sentence sl="jan li moku e ona." m="La homo manĝas ilin." />
  <Sentence sl="jan li alasa e kala, li seli e ona, li moku e ona." m="La homo ĉasas fiŝojn, kuiras ilin, manĝas ilin." />
</List>
