En leciono 8, ni lernis tri prepoziciojn: **󱥩 tawa**, **󱤬 lon**, **󱥧 tan**, kiuj ĉiuj temas pri loko kaj direkto. Tokipono fakte havas du prepoziciojn pli:
In lesson 8, we learned three prepositions: **󱥩 tawa**, **󱤬 lon**, **󱥧 tan**, which are all about location and direction. Toki Pona actually has two more prepositions:

<List>
  <Word sl="kepeken" m="per" />
  <Word sl="sama" m="kiel, kvazaŭ, simile al, same kiel" />
</List>

{/* A note for translators */}
{/*
  If your language has instrumental case, you can compare
  kepeken to instrumental case. But be careful: kepeken is
  *not* used when you are doing something alongside someone,
  e.g.: "I am walking with Emma."
*/}

Let's learn a few more words that go well with these prepositions:

<List>
  <Word sl="ante" m="ŝanĝi; malsama, alia" />
  <Word sl="pali" m="labori, konstrui" />
  <Word sl="leko" m="briko, bloko" />
</List>

{/* A note for translators */}
{/*
  Your language may have a word for "any activity". e.g.:
  "what are you *doing*? - I'm working. I'm sleeping. I'm eating."
  Please avoid translating "pali" with that word.
  "pali" is specifically "working", putting in effort.
*/}

Remember, prepositions start prepositional phrases:

<List>
  <Word sl="sama sina" m="same kiel vi" />
  <Word sl="kepeken toki" m="per lingvo" />
  <Word sl="kepeken ilo ante" m="per alia ilo" />
</List>

And those phrases then go either on the end of a sentence:

<List>
  <Sentence sl="mi toki sama sina." m="Mi parolas kiel vi." />
  <Sentence sl="mi pali e leko kepeken ilo." m="Mi faras brikojn per ilo." />
</List>

Or in place of the verb itself:

<List>
  <Sentence sl="ona li sama sina." m="Ili estas kiel vi." />
  <Sentence sl="ona li kepeken ilo." m="Ili uzas ilon." />
</List>

## Movi domon

Now that you know all five prepositions: **󱥩 tawa**, **󱤬 lon**, **󱥧 tan**, **󱤙 kepeken**, and **󱥖 sama**, here's something new about them: these can all be regular nouns, verbs, or adjectives!

<List>
  <Word sl="tawa" m="to move; movement; moving" />
  <Word sl="lon" m="vera, ekzistanta" />
  <Word sl="tan" m="kaŭzo, deveno" />
  <Word sl="kepeken" m="uzado" />
  <Word sl="sama" m="simila, sama" />
</List>

Some of them, like **󱥩 tawa**, **󱤬 lon**, are used this way very frequently. Others, like **󱥧 tan** and **󱤙 kepeken**, quite rarely. Let's look at some examples:

<List>
  <Sentence sl="sona mi li sama." m="My knowledge is similar." />
  <Sentence
    sl="toki sina li lon."
    m="Your speech is real.<br />(What you're saying is true.)"
  />
  <Sentence
    sl="mi wile lukin e sitelen tawa."
    m="I want to watch a moving picture. (a video, a film)"
  />
  <Sentence sl="mi sona ala e tan." m="I don't know the reason." />
</List>

We often like to shape sentences in such a way that it's impossible to misread these as "prepositions" -- notice how there's no phrase immediately after them!

Sometimes you can make very similar sentences, except one has a verb like **󱥩 tawa** or **󱤬 lon**, and the other has a preposition **󱥩 tawa** or **󱤬 lon**!
Make sure not to confuse these pairs:

<List>
  <Sentence
    sl="jan li tawa tomo."
    m="Iu iras al la domo. (prepozicio)"
  />
  <Sentence sl="jan li tawa e tomo." m="Iu movas la domon. (verbo)" />
  <Sentence sl="mi lon ma." m="Mi estas sur tero. (prepozicio)" />
  <Sentence
    sl="mi lon e ma."
    m="Mi ekzistigas teron.<br />Mi establas landon. (verbo)"
  />
</List>
