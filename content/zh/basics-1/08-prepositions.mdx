我们刚刚认识了一些国家，接着齐来讨论关于旅游吧！但是我们如何表达"前往"和"来自"的意思呢？首先，我们需要认识一些动词：

<List>
  <Word sl="weka" m="移除，离去；缺席，不在" />
  <Word sl="pana" m="给予，放置" />
  <Word sl="kama" m="过来，到达" />
  <Word sl="awen" m="停留，留下，保护" />
</List>

很多时候我们会在这些动词后遇到以下的介词：

<List>
  <Word sl="tawa" m="去，往" />
  <Word sl="lon" m="在，于" />
  <Word sl="tan" m="从，因为" />
</List>

{/* A note for translators */}
{/*
  If your language has noun case, please think about
  how "tawa, lon, tan" compare to your noun cases.
  The terms "dative case" and "locative case" are
  especially important to check.
*/}

*介词*放置于另外一组词语之前的时候，能让我们知道这词语和句子的关系。我们把介词跟这词语的组合称为*介词短语*：

<List>
  <Word sl="tawa soweli" m="往只动物" />
  <Word sl="lon ma" m="在地上，在...国家里" />
  <Word sl="tan tomo" m="从屋里" />
</List>

在道本语里，介词短语有两个用处。首先，我们可以把它放在句子的*后方*，来表示行动的位置、方向等等额外资讯：

<List>
  <Sentence sl="mi pana e waso tawa kasi." m="我把鸟儿放于树上。" />
  <Sentence sl="waso li awen lon kasi." m="鸟儿停留在树上。" />
  <Sentence
    sl="sina weka e waso tan kasi."
    m="你把鸟儿从树上移除。"
  />
</List>

Secondly, what if we want to talk about location or direction...without anything else happening? Fortunately, we can do that too. Prepositional phrases can come directly after **󱤧 li**, and act as the verb all by themselves. When prepositions act as verbs, they mean: **󱥩 tawa** 'to go to', **󱤬 lon** 'to be at', **󱥧 tan** 'to come from':
此外，我们怎么能单单表达某东西的位置或方向呢？为此，我们能把介词短语直接放在**󱤧 li**的后方，独自作为谓语。这时候介词们有以下的意思：**󱥩 tawa**'前往'，**󱤬 lon**'在'，**󱥧 tan**'来自'：

<List>
  <Sentence sl="jan Ema li tawa ma Nijon." m="Emma前往日本。" />
  <Sentence sl="jan Nina li lon ma Lusi." m="Nina正在俄罗斯。" />
  <Sentence sl="jan Mili li tan ma Kanse." m="Millie来自法国。" />
</List>

让我们看看更多示例： 

<List>
  <Sentence
    sl="mi tawa ma kasi."
    m="我前往植物的地方。<br />（可以是森林、草地、或者其他茂密的地方。）"
  />
  <Sentence
    sl="sona li weka tan mi."
    m="知识从我离去了。<br />（我忘记了。）"
  />
  <Sentence
    sl="soweli suwi li pona tawa mi."
    m="可爱的动物对我来说很好。<br />（我喜欢可爱的动物。）"
  />

{/* A note for translators */}
{/*
  Some languages have a verb "I like ...",
  other languages have a verb more like "to me it is nice ...".
  If your language is more like the second group,
  you might not need the text in brackets.
*/}

  <Sentence sl="jan Nowa li toki tawa jan Ema." m="Noah对Emma说话。" />
  <Sentence
    sl="jan li toki Tosi lon ma Tosi."
    m="人们在德国说德语。"
  />
</List>
